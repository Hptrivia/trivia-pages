Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert: How Difficulty Works in Our Quizzes

Every quiz on Trivia Gauntlet uses the same structure: 20 questions, split into four difficulty levels of five questions each. Questions appear in order, from easy to expert, so the quiz gets steadily harder as you go. This article explains what each level means, how the levels are chosen, and how to pick a quiz that suits how well you know a show.

Easy

Easy questions are about the things nearly every viewer remembers: the main characters, the setting, the famous catchphrases and the biggest plot points. If you have watched a series once, you should be able to answer most of them.

Medium

Medium questions ask for a little more attention. They cover supporting characters, jobs, relationships and events that a casual viewer might only half remember. They are where a regular fan starts to pull ahead.

Hard

Hard questions go into details: smaller characters, particular scenes and the finer points of the plot. People who have rewatched a series will do well here.

Expert

The expert questions are for true fans. They can cover the cast, the creators, release dates and the more unusual details of the show. Even so, each one has a clear, checkable answer, and it comes with an explanation after you pick.

A Real Example: Friends

Here is how the levels look in the Friends quiz. The easy question asks for the name of the coffee house where the group hangs out, which is Central Perk. A medium question asks for the name of Phoebe's identical twin sister, who is Ursula. A hard question asks what Chandler's mother does for a living, and the answer is that she writes romance novels. An expert question asks whose name Ross says at the altar when he marries Emily, and the answer is Rachel. Each step asks for a little more memory than the one before.

How a Question's Level Is Chosen

There are three things to consider. The first is how many viewers would know the answer without thinking. The second is how specific the detail is: a character's name is usually easier than the scene it appears in. The third is how believable the wrong options are, because a question with four close options is harder than one with two obvious fakes.

Levels are set by judgement, not by test data, so they are a guide and not a guarantee. A fan of one particular character may find a hard question easy, and the other way round.

Choosing a Quiz

If you love a show but you are not sure how well you know it, start with The Office, Friends or Stranger Things, where the early questions are gentle. If you want a stronger test, try Breaking Bad, The Wire or Attack on Titan, where the hard and expert levels cover a lot of detail.

What Your Score Means

A score of 15 or more out of 20 means you know the show very well. Ten to fourteen is a solid result for a regular viewer, and anything lower just means there is more to rewatch. Every quiz can be replayed, and the explanations help you remember the answers next time.

Tips for a Better Score

Read the explanation after every answer, even the ones you get right, because it often adds a small detail that helps with later questions. Replay a quiz after a few days and see what stuck. If you keep missing the hard level, that usually means you know the story well but not the smaller scenes, and a rewatch of your favourite season is the fastest fix.

Do the Levels Ever Change?

Yes. If a question turns out to be much easier or harder than its level suggests, or if it is unclear, it can be moved, reworded or replaced. If you notice one, please tell us on the contact page.
